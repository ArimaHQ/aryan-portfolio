Why ARIMA exists

Markets speak in signals, noise, stories and uncertainty.

People and businesses do not need more disconnected information. They need context: where a number came from, what it might mean, and how wrong it could be.

ARIMA brings evidence, models and financial workflows together and keeps the uncertainty visible. It is designed around decision intelligence. It is not a chatbot, a trading app or a portfolio tracker.

World II · Quantitative data & engineering

The work between a source and a signal.

ARIMA does its own sourcing and engineering. Upstream providers publish the original information. ARIMA collects it, checks it, aligns it, keeps its history and builds analysis on top, with every step traceable.

ARIMA does not own third-party data. Licence and display rights are recorded per source, and a provider credential alone never authorises showing its prices to customers.

What ARIMA adds

World III · Research, learning & exploration

Many possible futures. Each with its assumptions.

A simulation does not predict. It shows the range of outcomes a set of assumptions allows. Change an assumption and the range changes. That is the point.

Research Lab · model explorer

Calculated in your browser · synthetic history
Terminal distributionWhere prices end up after the period, across every simulated path.
P(finish below / above)Share of paths that end beyond the level on the final day.
P(touch)Share of paths that reach the level at any point. Touching is not finishing, so this is always at least as large.
Maximum drawdownThe largest fall from a running peak along a path. Shown as the median across paths.
VaR 95%The loss that 95% of paths do not exceed over the period.
Expected shortfall 95%The average loss in the worst 5% of paths. Always at least as large as VaR.

What the lab will not do

It never decides on its own that a regime change is happening. It produces a state and hands it on for synthesis. More paths make a smoother picture, not a more accurate forecast. Calibration and assumptions decide the answer.

Markets & behaviour

Numbers explain what happened. People explain why.

Prices move through economics, valuation, incentives, liquidity and regimes, and also through crowds, stories, fear, greed, overconfidence and loss aversion. ARIMA treats these as competing explanations to examine, not as facts.

Established concepts

What finance already knows

  • Valuation rests on assumptions about cash flows and discount rates
  • Liquidity and positioning change how prices react
  • Volatility clusters, and regimes shift
  • Loss aversion, herding and overconfidence shape decisions
ARIMA hypothesis

Context should reframe, not replace

  • Behavioural context can change how a conclusion is framed
  • It must never change the underlying calculation
  • Personal fit can only break a tie between options that already pass the financial test
Implemented locally

What is measured today

  • News sentiment from a local FinBERT classifier on public RSS
  • Liquidity-hunt and order-flow classifiers
  • Spending-habit baselines from entries a person provides
  • Bubble and regime states from price data

ARIMA cannot read emotions or intentions. It uses observable proxies such as headlines, price behaviour and the figures people choose to share, and it labels every interpretation as interpretation.

World IV · Command, coordination & practical use

A Brain that reasons. A layer that coordinates.

The Brain is ARIMA’s analytical core. The operating layer, Executive OS, runs the workflows around it: permissions, approvals, audit, voice and jobs. Today they are separate systems, and the connection between them is built but switched off.

Data sources feed the Brain’s collection and validation path. Inside the Brain, Left Brain calculations and Right Brain context meet in a Middle synthesis, supported by twelve family agents and a Research Lab. Executive OS sits beside the Brain with orchestration, permissions, approvals, audit, voice and jobs. Its read-only connection to the Brain is built but switched off. Live execution is switched off.

1 · Deterministic

Calculations

Valuation, risk, cash flow and loan schedules. The same inputs always give the same output and the same hash.

2 · Statistical

Models

Monte Carlo, regime states and a Bayesian confidence engine. Their assumptions are stated with every result.

3 · Language

Interpretation

A local language model explains why something was flagged and summarises meetings. It never changes the maths.

Every conclusion can be traced.

Results carry their inputs, assumptions, evidence identifiers, model version and a content hash. Confidence is a category that describes the evidence, not a probability. Stale, unknown or unverified evidence is not used.
